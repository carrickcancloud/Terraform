## 1. Key Services & Critical User Journeys

-   **Web Service:** The primary, user-facing website served through CloudFront, backed by the Web Tier ALB.
-   **Application Service:** The internal WordPress application logic served through an internal ALB, backed by the App Tier ASG.
-   **Database Service:** The backend MySQL database, a managed AWS RDS instance.
-   **Critical User Journey:** A user successfully loading the homepage and interacting with the core application features.

---

## 2. Service Level Indicators (SLIs)

SLIs are the quantitative metrics we use to measure the performance and health of our services. These are typically measured at the load balancer or application layer.

| SLI Name           | Metric                                     | Description                                                    |
| :----------------- | :----------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Availability**   | `(Success Requests / Total Requests) * 100`| The percentage of successful (HTTP 2xx, 3xx, 4xx) responses from the `web_tier` load balancer (measured at CloudFront edge or ALB). 5xx errors count against this metric. |
| **Latency (Web)**  | `p95 TargetResponseTime`                   | The 95th percentile latency of requests, measured at the `web_tier` load balancer. This means 95% of users experience this latency or better. |
| **Latency (App)**  | `p95 TargetResponseTime`                   | The 95th percentile latency of requests, measured at the `app_tier` internal load balancer. This reflects internal service communication speed. |
| **Error Rate**     | `(5xx Errors / Total Requests) * 100`      | The percentage of server-side errors (HTTP 5xx) generated by the `web_tier` or `app_tier`. |

---

## 3. Service Level Objectives (SLOs) & Error Budgets

SLOs are our specific reliability targets over a rolling 30-day window. An Error Budget is the maximum allowable downtime or performance degradation for a service over a given period without violating the SLO.

| SLI                | SLO Target                  | Error Budget (30-day rolling window)         |
| :----------------- | :-------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| **Availability**   | **99.9%**                   | We can have **~43 minutes** of downtime per month (or equivalent error rate). |
| **Latency (Web)**  | **95% of requests < 500ms** | **5%** of requests can be slower than 500ms. |
| **Latency (App)**  | **99% of requests < 200ms** | **1%** of requests can be slower than 200ms. |

### SLO Alarms

-   **Latency SLO Burn Alarms:** CloudWatch alarms are configured (e.g., `web_slo_latency_burn`) to monitor key metrics. These alarms are typically less sensitive than immediate outage alerts and are designed to notify the team when they are at risk of violating their monthly error budget, allowing for proactive intervention.
-   **Error Budget Policy:** If error budgets are being consumed too quickly, the team may need to defer new feature development to focus on reliability work.
